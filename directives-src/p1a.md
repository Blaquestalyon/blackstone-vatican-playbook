### WBS: P1.A.1.1
:: SUMMARY
Stand up the legal foundation for the whole program. Retain securities counsel and lock the legal form and jurisdiction of the SMA program before anything else gets built on top of it.
:: DIRECTIVE
This is the first domino. Every downstream item (ADV, custody, GIPS, EU routing) inherits the entity and jurisdiction chosen here, so treat it as a structural decision, not a paperwork errand.

**Actions**
1. Shortlist 2-3 law firms with genuine registered-investment-adviser and separately-managed-account experience. Prioritize firms that have taken values-screened or faith-based mandates through to institutional allocation, because they will understand the Vatican end-buyer.
2. Run a conflicts check and request written fee proposals. Push for a fixed or capped fee on the formation package rather than open hourly billing.
3. Scope the engagement letter to cover: adviser/entity structure, the SMA program's legal form, jurisdiction selection, IMA drafting, and the registration strategy (Item P1.D).
4. Put the three structural questions to counsel in writing: (a) which jurisdiction the adviser is domiciled in, (b) whether the SMA program runs under an existing adviser entity or a newly formed one, and (c) how EU distribution will later attach, so AIFMD implications are flagged now rather than discovered in Phase 4.

**Deliverable:** signed engagement letter plus a one-page legal-structure memo stating the recommended form and jurisdiction with reasoning.

**Done when:** counsel is formally engaged and the structure/jurisdiction recommendation is documented and ready for Kate's review at P1.A.1.3.

> The jurisdiction choice cascades into tax, ADV filing, and EU routing. Exact domicile depends on Kate's residency, any existing entities, and the target investor base. Confirm with counsel; do not assume a default.

### WBS: P1.A.1.2
:: SUMMARY
Counsel drafts the Investment Management Agreement and the SMA account terms. This is the contract that governs how the fund manages client money.
:: DIRECTIVE
The IMA is the operating spine of the SMA relationship. Get it drafted to institutional standard now, because gatekeepers and the Vatican will read it during diligence.

**Actions**
1. Have counsel draft the IMA and the standard SMA account terms in parallel with the structure memo, using the form and jurisdiction from P1.A.1.1.
2. Make sure the draft addresses, at minimum: scope of discretionary authority, the values/ethical screening mandate (cross-reference the methodology in P1.B so the contract and the rulebook agree), fee schedule and calculation, custody arrangement, reporting cadence, standard of care and liability, termination, and assignment.
3. Confirm the agreement can accommodate an institutional/Vatican-scale client without a rewrite later (large-ticket terms, side-letter mechanics, most-favored-nation language).
4. Circulate the draft internally and to any operations resource who will actually administer these accounts, so operational reality is baked in before signing.

**Deliverable:** clean IMA draft plus SMA account-terms template, ready for principal review.

**Done when:** counsel delivers a review-ready draft that reflects the screening mandate and institutional scalability.

### WBS: P1.A.1.3
:: SUMMARY
Kate reviews and approves the final IMA and structure terms. This is a strategic decision she owns; it sets the fund's legal risk posture.
:: DIRECTIVE
**Kate only — DECIDE.** This cannot be delegated. The terms she approves here define what the fund is contractually on the hook for.

**Actions**
1. Kate reads the IMA and structure memo in full, not a summary. She is the one who lives with the standard of care, liability, and discretion language.
2. She confirms the screening mandate in the contract matches how she actually intends to run the portfolio. Any gap between the contract and her real process is a future compliance problem.
3. She lists any changes and sends them back to counsel; iterate until she is satisfied.
4. She gives explicit written approval to proceed to signature.

**Done when:** Kate has approved the final structure and IMA terms in writing, with all her changes incorporated.

### WBS: P1.A.1.4
:: SUMMARY
Kate executes (signs) the IMA and the governing agreements. Compliance requires her own hand.
:: DIRECTIVE
**Kate only — SIGN.** Purely a principal act; nobody can sign for her.

**Actions**
1. Counsel prepares final execution versions of the IMA and any governing/organizational agreements.
2. Kate signs (wet or qualified e-signature as counsel directs) and dates each.
3. Store fully executed originals in the fund's permanent records; distribute counterparts as required.

**Done when:** all governing agreements are fully executed and filed. This closes the legal-form foundation and unblocks account opening.

### WBS: P1.A.2.1
:: SUMMARY
Prepare the account-opening and trading-authorization documents for the live SMA. This turns the legal structure into accounts that can actually hold and trade assets.
:: DIRECTIVE
Bridge from paper to a functioning account. This item is gated by custodian and administrator onboarding (Item P1.C), so sequence accordingly.

**Actions**
1. Collect each counterparty's account-opening packet (custodian, executing brokers, administrator) and build a single checklist of every form and every required signature.
2. Complete the entity and adviser sections, attach the executed IMA and organizational documents, and pre-fill everything that does not require Kate's signature.
3. Prepare the trading-authorization / limited-power-of-attorney documents that grant the adviser discretionary trading authority over the SMA accounts.
4. Assemble beneficial-ownership and authorized-signer information consistent with the AML/KYC program (Item P1.D.2) so custody onboarding does not stall.

**Deliverable:** a complete, signature-ready account-opening and trading-authorization package.

**Done when:** every document is filled and staged, needing only Kate's signatures (P1.A.2.3) to activate.

### WBS: P1.A.2.2
:: SUMMARY
Configure the order-management and trade-routing setup for the SMA accounts, so orders can be placed, routed, and settled correctly.
:: DIRECTIVE
This is the operational plumbing that lets the strategy trade. Get it right before any live capital arrives.

**Actions**
1. Stand up or configure the order-management system (OMS) and connect it to the executing brokers and the custodian for straight-through processing.
2. Map trade routing: which venues/brokers, allocation logic across accounts, and how fills flow to the custodian and administrator for settlement and NAV.
3. Build the screening rules (Item P1.B) into the pre-trade compliance layer so excluded securities are blocked at order entry, not caught after the fact.
4. Run end-to-end test trades in a staging or paper environment: order entry, routing, fill, allocation, custody booking, and administrator capture.

**Deliverable:** a configured, tested OMS/routing setup with pre-trade screening enforced.

**Done when:** a test order flows cleanly from entry to custody-and-admin booking, and excluded names are provably blocked pre-trade.

> Do not skip the pre-trade screening integration. A single excluded holding slipping into a Catholic-values portfolio is exactly the failure the Vatican buyer is screening for.

### WBS: P1.A.2.3
:: SUMMARY
Kate signs the trading authorizations and account-control agreements. Her signature grants the adviser trading authority and confirms account control.
:: DIRECTIVE
**Kate only — SIGN.** She is granting discretionary authority and attesting to account control; only she can do it.

**Actions**
1. Review the staged trading-authorization and account-control agreements (from P1.A.2.1) for accuracy of account numbers, entity names, and authority scope.
2. Kate signs each authorization and any control agreements the custodian requires.
3. Return executed documents to the custodian/brokers and confirm the accounts move to active/tradeable status.

**Done when:** signed authorizations are lodged with the counterparties and the SMA accounts are live and tradeable.
